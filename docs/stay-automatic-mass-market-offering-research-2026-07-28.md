# Stay Automatic mass-market offering — competitor research and product plan

Date: 2026-07-28

## Working thesis

Stay Automatic should not lead as “we will install Hermes for you.” Kyle is right: for a motivated buyer, the literal install/setup path can often be reduced to copy/paste guidance from Hermes and the docs. The paid value is not the command sequence. The paid value is orientation, taste, trust, workflow selection, safe credential posture, and the confidence to avoid wasting months bouncing between ChatGPT, Zapier, AI-agent SaaS, prompt packs, and guru courses.

The best mass-market wedge is therefore:

> A practical field guide and guided quiz that helps a normal person choose and set up their own AI operating layer — starting simple, avoiding lock-in, and escalating to Stay Automatic setup/consulting only when their real workflow warrants it.

## Competitive map

### 1. Default AI assistants with connectors

**Examples**
- ChatGPT apps/plugins/connectors: https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11487775-connectors-in-chatgpt
- Claude Google Workspace connectors: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors
- Google Workspace/Gemini: https://workspace.google.com/solutions/ai/ and https://cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise
- Microsoft 365 Copilot/Copilot Studio: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/pricing

**Threat level: very high for mainstream users.**

These are the default path for almost everyone. They win on trust, brand recognition, native app access, low perceived setup risk, and polished UX. A normal business owner will ask: “Why not just use ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini with Gmail and Drive connected?”

**Stay Automatic counter-position**
- Do not pretend they are bad. Say: “If you only need a better chat box inside your existing account, start there.”
- Hermes/open-source harness wins when the user wants an agent that lives outside one vendor, works over Telegram/Slack/email/terminal, can swap models/providers, use real tools, run scheduled jobs, preserve skills/memory, and operate across systems rather than inside one assistant’s product boundary.
- Strongest phrase: **own your operating layer, rent the models.**

### 2. Automation platforms adding agents

**Examples**
- Zapier pricing/features: https://zapier.com/pricing — AI orchestration, Zaps, Tables, Forms, MCP, agents; free, Professional from $19.99/mo, Team from $69/mo.
- Make: https://www.make.com/en/pricing
- n8n: https://n8n.io/pricing/ — cloud from roughly low tens/mo, self-hostable.
- Bardeen: https://www.bardeen.ai/pricing
- Relay.app: shut-down notice found at https://relay.app/ — useful cautionary tale about platform risk.

**Threat level: high for workflow automation, medium for personal agent experience.**

Zapier/Make/n8n are much easier to explain to business owners: “connect app A to app B.” Zapier especially has the distribution and trust. n8n has the open-source/self-hostable audience.

**Stay Automatic counter-position**
- Automation platforms are excellent for stable if-this-then-that workflows. Hermes is better when the user wants a conversational operator with memory, tools, approvals, file/browser/terminal access, and reusable procedures.
- Do not fight Zapier where Zapier is best. Teach people when to use Zapier/n8n versus Hermes. This increases trust.
- Position Stay Automatic as the layer that helps choose the right automation substrate, not as a cult around one tool.

### 3. No-code AI agent builders

**Examples**
- Lindy: https://www.lindy.ai/ — “ultimate AI executive assistant,” Plus reported at $49.99/mo in search result; admin work, inbox, meetings, scheduling.
- Gumloop: https://www.gumloop.com/pricing — Pro starts at $37/mo, unlimited seats, agents/workflows, credits; enterprise controls.
- Relevance AI: https://relevanceai.com/agents — agent workforce positioning.
- MindStudio: https://www.mindstudio.ai/ — build AI agents; Individual around $20/mo plus usage; “no surcharges/vendor lock-in” messaging.
- Agent.ai: https://agent.ai/pricing — marketplace of agents, $25/mo Pro or $10/mo individual agents.
- Manus: https://manus.im/pricing — broad autonomous task execution, credits, websites/slides/research/browser operator.

**Threat level: high for demos and ads; medium for long-term ownership.**

These products look slick and are usually easier to start than Hermes. They will beat Stay Automatic on instant UI polish and one-click onboarding.

**Stay Automatic counter-position**
- Again, do not call them fake. Many are useful.
- Push the ownership angle: their agent lives in their cloud, their pricing, their action limits, their roadmap, and their shutdown/acquisition risk.
- Use Relay.app shutting down as a sober, non-fearmongering example: tools change; your workflow architecture should survive.

### 4. Open-source AI app/agent frameworks

**Examples**
- Dify: https://dify.ai/ — open-source LLM apps/workflows.
- Open WebUI: https://openwebui.com/ — self-hosted AI platform, connect to local/cloud models.
- Flowise, LangChain/LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGPT, Composio: developer-heavy ecosystem; Composio offers 1,000+ integrations for agents: https://composio.dev/

**Threat level: high for technical users, low for nontechnical buyers.**

Technical people can piece this together themselves. Nontechnical people will drown in options.

**Stay Automatic counter-position**
- “We are not selling you another framework. We are giving you a map.”
- Hermes is a strong centerpiece because it is already an end-user agent harness: memory, skills, tools, messaging, cron, browser, files, terminal, MCP, multiple providers.
- The guide should explicitly compare Hermes with n8n, Dify, Open WebUI, and Zapier in plain English.

### 5. AI automation agencies and consultants

**Examples / market signal**
- AI agency pricing articles commonly cite small-business setup fees around $1,500–$5,000 and retainers around $500–$1,500/mo; broader custom builds $2,500–$15,000+.
- AAA Accelerator: https://www.aaaaccelerator.com/ — 7,150+ members claimed, split tracks for builders and business owners, “build/sell/deploy AI systems.”
- Many Skool/YouTube AI automation agency communities teach the agency model rather than sell sober operator education.

**Threat level: high in ad auctions and hype language; low in trust if Stay Automatic stays grounded.**

This space is noisy. A lot of “AI agency” messaging is guru-coded: $10k/mo, automate everything, start an agency, vague screenshots.

**Stay Automatic counter-position**
- Kyle has actual local proof. Use it carefully: roofing, pizza, snorkel/photo ops, claim/admin workflows, setup fee proof.
- Tone should be anti-hype but not timid: “AI agents are useful. The internet is making them stupid. Here is the practical setup.”
- Lead with lived operator credibility: small businesses, messy access, phone/Telegram workflows, actual approvals, actual deliverables.

### 6. Info products, prompt packs, templates, and free guides

**Examples**
- OpenAI Academy small-business prompt pack: https://academy.openai.com/public/clubs/small-business-ipf4m/resources/run-your-small-business-with-chatgpt-2025-11-18
- U.S. Chamber AI training guide for small business: https://www.uschamber.com/co/start/strategy/ai-training-guide-small-business
- Etsy prompt packs and PDF guides often sell in the $3–$30 range.

**Threat level: high on price, low on depth.**

Cheap prompt packs anchor customer expectations low. Free business AI guides from trusted institutions can answer basic “what is AI?” questions.

**Stay Automatic counter-position**
- Do not sell a prompt pack. Sell a setup map + decision system + practical operating manual.
- Include prompts, but make them a small part of the product.
- The PDF should feel like a field manual, not a Canva ebook.

## Ranked competitor threats

1. **ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini with native connectors** — biggest mainstream substitute.
2. **Zapier/Make/n8n** — best-understood automation alternative.
3. **Lindy/Gumloop/MindStudio/Relevance AI** — slick no-code agent products.
4. **AI automation agencies/courses** — competing for attention and ads, but often easier to differentiate against with honest positioning.
5. **Dify/Open WebUI/LangChain/CrewAI ecosystem** — threat for technical audience; opportunity as comparison content.
6. **Prompt packs/free small-business guides** — price anchor and SEO/ad competitor, not direct value competitor.

## Pushback on Kyle’s current assumptions

### Correct instinct

Kyle is right that selling “I will install Hermes for you” as the core product is too thin. It can become disingenuous if the setup is mostly guided copy/paste.

### But do not underprice the real work

The real work is not install commands. The real work is:
- deciding what should and should not be automated,
- getting accounts/invites/OAuth/secrets configured safely,
- turning vague business pain into concrete workflows,
- writing durable skills/prompts/context,
- setting approval gates,
- verifying the system actually works,
- being there when the client hits a scary setup screen.

That is legitimate paid value.

### The quiz is good, but not as a gimmick

The quiz should not be “what AI tool are you?” It should be a triage engine that earns trust by sometimes saying:
- “Just use ChatGPT Plus for now.”
- “Use Zapier/n8n, not an agent.”
- “You need a human-reviewed setup because this touches customers/money/regulated decisions.”
- “Hermes is worth it because you need persistent agent operations across apps and channels.”

### Open source is not enough

“No lock-in” matters to thoughtful buyers but is abstract to normal people. Translate it into outcomes:
- Your workflows survive model changes.
- Your agent can live in Telegram/Slack/email, not just one vendor’s chat window.
- You can use multiple models and tools.
- Your procedures can become portable skills.
- You can own the system instead of rebuilding every time a SaaS plan changes.

## Recommended offer stack

### Free layer: AI Workstyle Quiz

Goal: qualify users and give immediate value.

Quiz dimensions:
1. Work type: owner/operator, sales, admin, trades, creator, student, dev/technical, manager.
2. App ecosystem: Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Apple/iCloud, Slack/Discord/Telegram, CRM/POS/booking tools.
3. Pain mode: inbox, scheduling, documents, research, customer follow-up, reporting, content, data entry, project tracking.
4. Risk level: customer-facing, money movement, legal/medical/financial, public posting, internal-only.
5. Technical comfort: copy/paste commands, browser setup only, willing to screen-share, wants done-for-you.
6. Agent ambition: smarter chat, repeatable automations, true operator in messaging, business process assistant.

Outputs:
- Recommended starting stack: ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini vs Zapier/n8n vs Hermes vs consult.
- First 3 workflows to try.
- “Do not automate yet” warning list.
- CTA to buy the guide or book setup.

### Paid entry product: The Stay Automatic AI Operating Layer Field Guide

Suggested price: **$29–$79**.

Positioning: not a prompt pack; a decision and setup manual.

Contents:
1. Plain-English AI tool map.
2. When to use ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini alone.
3. When to use Zapier/Make/n8n.
4. When an open-source harness like Hermes makes sense.
5. Hermes setup path: desktop/CLI/cloud/VPS/messaging in practical terms.
6. Connecting work apps safely: Gmail/Calendar/Drive/Sheets/Docs, Slack/Discord/Telegram, files, browser, web search.
7. Model/provider/no-lock-in guide.
8. Memory/skills/project notes: what belongs where.
9. Approval gates: customer messages, money, public posts, legal/regulated decisions.
10. 20 practical starter workflows by role.
11. Setup checklists and troubleshooting.
12. Upgrade path: when to ask for help.

### Mid-tier: Guided Setup Kit

Suggested price: **$149–$299**.

Includes:
- Guide.
- Prebuilt setup worksheets.
- Copy/paste Hermes commands.
- Starter skill/context templates.
- 30–60 minute group setup workshop or recorded walkthrough.
- Community/office-hours access for 30 days.

### Done-with-you setup

Suggested price: **$500–$1,500** depending on scope.

Includes:
- 1–2 hour live setup call.
- Hermes/AI assistant running on preferred channel.
- 1–3 low-risk workflows configured.
- Credential/integration checklist.
- Safety/approval gates.
- Hand-off notes.

### Done-for-you business implementation

Suggested price: **$1,500–$5,000 setup + $500–$1,500/mo retainer**.

Scope only when there is real workflow complexity. Keep current consulting path here.

### New details to fold into positioning

1. **AI-agency/course competitors are mostly seller-side, not owner-side.** Liam Ottley/AAA Accelerator, Nick Saraev/Maker School, Skool groups, and agency-in-a-box offers teach people to sell AI automation. Stay Automatic should explicitly own the inverse: practical AI setup for owners/operators who want leverage in their own work, not another “start an AI agency” hustle.

2. **Closest SMB owner-side competitors are managed AI employees and local implementation firms.** Cyndra-like offers around ~$50/mo create a low-price anchor, while boutique consultants quote thousands for assessments/builds. This supports a ladder: free quiz → $47–$97 guide → $197 triage → $1.5k–$3.5k setup sprint → larger implementation only when warranted.

3. **No-lock-in alone is not enough.** n8n, Dify, Flowise, OpenWebUI, LangGraph, CrewAI, and AutoGPT can also claim open/self-hostable/model-flexible. Stay Automatic’s sharper claim is *guided portability*: choose models, connect work apps, keep files/workflows inspectable, use local/terminal/MCP/messaging channels, and avoid betting the whole operation on one chat app or SaaS builder.

4. **Competitors’ strongest counterclaims are legitimate:** no setup required, native connectors, visual/no-code collaboration, enterprise governance, managed reliability, and huge integration catalogs. The guide should concede these honestly and teach when to choose them.

5. **Prompt packs are commoditized.** Etsy/Gumroad/PromptBase/OpenAI Academy make prompt packs feel cheap/free. Prompts should be bonuses, not the core product. The core product is judgment, workflow triage, approval gates, and a working setup path.

6. **Best quiz framing from funnel research:** call it `Workflow Leak Quiz`, `AI Fit Check`, or `Stay Automatic Time-Back Scorecard`. Segment users into `Prompt-first`, `Simple automation`, `Agent-ready`, and `Not ready yet`.

7. **Best guarantee:** “If this does not help you identify one workflow worth improving — or one automation you should avoid — reply within 30 days for a refund.” This is honest and aligned with anti-hype positioning.
