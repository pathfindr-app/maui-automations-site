# Tony Simons Hermes Masterclass — ethical competitive analysis

Date: 2026-07-29
Source reviewed: public X post/article roadmap at https://x.com/tonysimons_/status/2079066422211117218

## What the public article offers
Tony Simons published a free twelve-part technical roadmap covering the Hermes agent loop, deployment and persistence, memory and skills, toolsets, cron, messaging gateways, delegation, browser/computer use, Kanban coordination, profiles/admin, and operational pitfalls.

Its strengths are technical depth, a complete infrastructure map, credibility with existing Hermes users, and a strong “whole system” framing. Public engagement on the source includes a reader suggesting an ebook or Gumroad product, which indicates demand for packaged learning.

## What not to copy
Do not reproduce the article text, examples, screenshots, title, twelve-part sequence, or close paraphrases as a Stay Automatic product. Do not market his research as ours. Link or attribute the public source if directly discussing it.

General facts about Hermes features can be independently researched from official Hermes documentation and demonstrated through our own installations. Our product should use original explanations, examples, exercises, templates, visuals, and case material.

## Market gap for Stay Automatic
The masterclass primarily explains Hermes as infrastructure to readers already motivated to understand the system. Stay Automatic can serve a different customer and job:

- Owner/operators rather than agent-framework enthusiasts.
- Workflow diagnosis before technical education.
- “What should I automate first?” rather than “How does every Hermes subsystem work?”
- Real small-business examples and approval design.
- A guided first implementation rather than a comprehensive architecture survey.
- Honest recommendations to use plain ChatGPT or deterministic automation when a persistent operator is unnecessary.

## Original offer direction
### Free
An AI Operator Fit Check or conversational workflow diagnostic that returns:
- the first workflow worth testing,
- the appropriate tool class,
- the human approval point,
- one thing not to automate yet.

### Entry product
An original owner-operator field manual built around decisions and implementation:
1. Find the workflow leak.
2. Decide whether chat, deterministic automation, or an operator fits.
3. Draw the workflow and approval boundary.
4. Choose where the operator lives and what it may access.
5. Connect one channel and one tool safely.
6. Turn the procedure into a reusable operating instruction.
7. Test failures, handoffs, and recovery.
8. Measure whether it saved time or improved follow-up.
9. Expand only after the first lane works.

### Service ladder
- Workflow map by email or diagnostic.
- Guided setup for one bounded workflow.
- Done-with-you operator setup with approvals and handoff notes.
- Business implementation only when cross-app complexity justifies it.

## Better marketing angle
Avoid competing on “the most complete Hermes course.” Compete on getting a normal owner from scattered pain to one safe, working result.

Potential lines:
- “Before you automate everything, find the one workflow worth fixing.”
- “Chat is useful. An operator is for work that has somewhere to go.”
- “One repeated workflow. One approval point. One working result.”
- “Not another AI course—a map for putting AI to work in your actual business.”

## Proof moat
Use Stay Automatic’s own field experience: roofing report and claim administration, restaurant operations, customer photo fulfillment, Google Workspace, social preparation, and after-hours lead handling. Show process diagrams, before/after handoffs, safety decisions, and tested artifacts. Never invent metrics or imply a client result that was not measured.

## Monetization principle
The defensible value is not information that already exists publicly. It is diagnosis, prioritization, safe configuration, original workflow templates, verification, and support when real accounts and messy business processes are involved.
