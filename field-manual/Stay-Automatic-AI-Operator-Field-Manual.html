AI Operator Field Manual

A note from the field

This manual starts with work—not AI.

Most businesses do not need “automation everywhere.” They need one repeated handoff to stop leaking time, attention, or revenue.

The first win is not an all-knowing agent. It is one bounded lane that works when Monday gets messy.

What this will help you do

  • Find the first workflow worth improving.
  • Choose between ordinary AI chat, a fixed automation, and a persistent operator.
  • Define what AI may draft, prepare, or do.
  • Build a human approval point that makes sense.
  • Run a seven-day pilot and decide whether to expand.

What this will not do

  • Promise that AI can run your whole business.
  • Turn bad processes into good ones by magic.
  • Ask you to paste passwords into a worksheet.
  • Replace professional judgment in regulated work.
  • Sell you software before the job is clear.

Use professional judgment

This manual is educational. Customer commitments, money movement, legal/insurance/medical/financial decisions, public claims, account permissions, and sensitive data require appropriate human review and professional advice.

The workflow leak

Do not automate pain you cannot name.

A workflow leak is a repeated point where work waits, gets retyped, loses context, or depends on someone remembering.

Look for these signals

  • “I thought someone replied.”
  • Information copied between email, texts, and a sheet.
  • Files renamed or sorted by hand every day.
  • Morning cleanup caused by after-hours activity.
  • A report rebuilt from the same ingredients.
  • A customer asking for status your team already knows.
  • Work that stalls when one person is away.

Do not confuse these with a workflow

  • “We need AI.”
  • “We should automate marketing.”
  • “I want an agent that does everything.”
  • “Our team needs to be more efficient.”
  • A feature list copied from an AI product page.
Good target: “Every missed call after 5 PM should create a callback task and receive an approved acknowledgment within five minutes.”

ChooseThree tool classes
Use the smallest class that fits

Chat · Automation · Operator

Not every problem needs an operator.

CLASS 01

AI chat

Best for occasional writing, research, analysis, brainstorming, and transforming information while you remain in the conversation.

Use when: the work is ad hoc and does not need to persist across systems.

CLASS 02

Fixed automation

Best for predictable “when X happens, do Y” handoffs between apps.

Use when: the rules are stable and judgment is limited. Think Zapier, Make, or n8n.

CLASS 03

AI operator

Best when work repeats across messages, files, browser tasks, schedules, or apps and benefits from context and judgment.

Use when: the job needs memory, tools, procedures, and approvals.

The honest answer earns trust

If two social posts a month are the whole job, ordinary AI chat is probably enough. If every inbound lead must be captured, qualified, scheduled, and summarized across systems, an operator may be justified.

Inputs: caller number, timestamp, source, approved message.

Decision: existing customer, new lead, spam, or uncertain.

Example · Photo delivery

Trigger: completed customer photo sale.

Inputs: customer identity, email, order, trip date.

Decision: paid, duplicate, missing email, or ready.

Action: create folder, stage export, prepare delivery.

Record: folder path, upload status, delivery timestamp.

The record matters. If the system cannot show what it saw, what it decided, and what it did, you cannot supervise or improve it.

Human control

Approval is a product feature.

The goal is not “human in the loop” everywhere. It is the right human at the moment the consequence becomes real.

Risk levelExamplesDefault posture
LowSort internal files, summarize notes, prepare drafts, classify routine items.May run automatically with logs and review sampling.
MediumCustomer replies, scheduling, public post drafts, status updates.Prepare and queue; require approval until accuracy is proven.
HighMoney, contracts, coverage/claims, regulated decisions, credentials, destructive account changes.Human decides and executes; AI may gather or draft only.

Good approval packet

  • What triggered the action.
  • The source information used.
  • The proposed action or message.
  • Why it chose this path.
  • Approve, edit, or reject.

Weak approval packet

  • “AI recommends approval.”
  • No source link or customer context.
  • One giant summary with hidden details.
  • No way to edit before sending.
  • No record of who approved.

Access posture

Give the workflow the smallest set of keys.

Prefer

  • OAuth authorization instead of passwords.
  • App-specific roles or service accounts.
  • Read-only before write access.
  • One folder, inbox label, calendar, or workspace.
  • Revocable access with a named owner.
  • Separate test and production destinations.

Avoid

  • Passwords pasted into chat or worksheets.
  • Full admin rights “just in case.”
  • Shared personal accounts.
  • Unlogged destructive actions.
  • Credentials stored in prompts or documents.
  • Access no one knows how to revoke.
SystemNeeded accessOwnerHow revoked

Never write a password here

This page is for access planning. Use OAuth, administrator invites, or an approved secret manager. If setup is confusing, do it live with the account owner present.

Information boundary

Not all context belongs in the operator.

Separate what is needed to complete the workflow from what is merely available.

Needed

The minimum customer, job, file, message, or schedule context required for the lane.

Helpful

Context that improves quality but can be withheld during the first pilot.

Off-limits

Passwords, payment details, unrelated customer records, highly sensitive data, or anything outside the approved scope.

Retention question

For every input and output, ask: does it need to be stored, where, for how long, and who can delete it? “The AI remembers it” is not a data policy.

Choose the channel by the work

  • Messaging: quick requests, approvals, alerts.
  • Email: inbound jobs, documents, client context.
  • Form: structured intake and repeatability.
  • Schedule: recurring checks and reports.
  • Dashboard: review queues and exceptions.

Do not add a new inbox casually

If the team lives in email and text, an elegant new dashboard may become one more place nobody checks. Start with the surface people already trust, then add a control panel only when it improves supervision.

BuildKnowledge and procedures
Teach the lane, not the whole company

The field brief

Give the operator a short, inspectable source of truth.

A useful procedure is not a giant prompt. It is a compact operating instruction with evidence and boundaries.

Include

  1. Purpose of the workflow.
  2. Trigger and expected result.
  3. Definitions and source locations.
  4. Decision rules with examples.
  5. Allowed tools and actions.
  6. Approval and escalation rules.
  7. What success looks like.

Exclude

  1. Passwords or secret tokens.
  2. Vague brand mythology.
  3. Unverified claims and metrics.
  4. Rules for unrelated departments.
  5. Every possible edge case on day one.
  6. Instructions that conflict without priority.
  7. “Use your best judgment” with no boundary.

Procedure test

Could a careful new employee follow this lane, know when to stop, and show you what happened? If not, the operator will not rescue the ambiguity.

Recovery plan

The operator should fail visibly and reversibly.

For each action, decide

  • How do we know it completed?
  • What does partial completion look like?
  • Can the action be reversed?
  • What work must be preserved?
  • Who receives the alert?
  • When does the system stop retrying?

Preflight

Do not call it live until these are true.

  • The trigger is observable and tested.
  • The source of truth is named.
  • Workflow scope fits in one paragraph.
  • Access is minimum necessary.
  • Secrets are outside prompts and documents.
  • Customer-facing actions require approval.
  • Every action leaves a record.
  • Missing information triggers a stop.
  • Ambiguous identity triggers escalation.
  • Duplicate jobs are handled safely.
  • Tool failure preserves partial work.
  • An exception owner is available.
  • The workflow can be disabled quickly.
  • The seven-day measure is written down.

Pattern 05Workspace and social preparation
Useful drafts · Guard public actions

Field pattern

The operator as a preparation layer.

Start read/draft only

Google Workspace lane

Find approved files, summarize an email thread, prepare a meeting packet, draft a reply, update a controlled sheet, or produce a daily open-items summary.

Approval boundary: sending, deletion, external sharing, calendar commitments, and sensitive-document access.

Social preparation lane

Match a real photo to an approved content theme, draft captions, create platform variants, prepare a queue, and flag missing price/hour/offer confirmation.

Approval boundary: final publish, prices, specials, hours, customer images, and public claims.

The preparation principle

Let the operator remove searching, formatting, and first-draft labor before giving it irreversible authority. Draft quality can be sampled. A deleted file or false public offer is harder to recover.
